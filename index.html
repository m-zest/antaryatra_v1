The method

Three bodies. The city outside, the self inside — walked together.

Every exploration on this journey has two faces. You walk the city, and you walk yourself — the same territory, seen from opposite sides. They meet at a single point the old texts called the interface. In Varanasi, this deepens across three days: you gather three times daily — noon, afternoon and evening — and each gathering climbs one layer higher, from the body you can touch to the self underneath everything.

Why this exists

Most people are too busy living to notice they've stopped asking the real questions.

Work, notifications, small talk, repeat. Who am I becoming — what am I avoiding — what do I actually want. All of it gets pushed to later. Later rarely comes.

Antaryatra takes that later and makes it now — for nine days, across four of the places in India that have spent centuries being honest about what a life is for.

The route

Four stops. The direction is always inward.

Days 1–2 · Stillness

Rishikesh

In the Himalayan foothills, where the Ganga is still young and fast. Mornings of yoga and quiet, evenings by the river. The first two days aren't about answers — they're about slowing down enough to hear the questions.

  • Dawn practice above the river, before the town wakes
  • An evening walk across Lakshman Jhula as the lamps come on
  • Night one: the first conversation
Days 3–4 · Letting go

Bodh Gaya

Where the Buddha sat beneath a tree until he stopped running from himself. Long, silent sits in the Mahabodhi gardens. This is where the trip stops being comfortable, and starts being useful.

  • Morning silence beneath the descendants of the Bodhi tree
  • Circling the temple at dusk with pilgrims from a dozen countries
  • An evening on what you're still carrying
Days 5–6 · Confluence

Prayagraj

At the Triveni Sangam, three rivers meet — one of them said to be invisible. A quiet boat out to the confluence, and a question worth sitting with: which parts of your own life have stopped speaking to each other?

  • A rowing boat out to where the waters change colour
  • Gulls, pilgrims, and a horizon with no edges
  • An evening on the parts of you that drifted apart
Days 7–9 · Death and rebirth

Varanasi

The oldest living city on earth, and where the whole journey gathers itself. Sunrise on the Ganges, the ghats, the evening aarti at Dashashwamedh — and three days of the deepest work, meeting at three points of the self from noon to dusk.

  • On the water before sunrise, the city waking behind you
  • Three daily checkpoints — noon, afternoon, evening
  • Night nine: the last conversation

Every night

Two things happen every night.

The day's walking is done by dark. What's left is the part most trips skip entirely — sitting with what the day stirred up, and then setting it down before sleep.

One · the private hour

One hour, one-on-one with your guide. You share the whole day — inner and outer, city and self — and mostly, you just talk. Here's roughly how the questions build, city by city.

Rishikesh

“Where are you, really — not where you're supposed to be?”

Bodh Gaya

“What are you still carrying that you could put down?”

Prayagraj

“Which parts of you have stopped talking to each other?”

Varanasi

“If this were genuinely your last week, what would you do differently?”

Two · the discourse

Then the group gathers for a short discourse that scales the whole day back down to size — so you end each night finished, not carrying it into the next.

Why this exists

Most people are too busy living to notice they've stopped asking the real questions.

Work, notifications, small talk, repeat. Who am I becoming — what am I avoiding — what do I actually want. All of it gets pushed to later. Later rarely comes.

Antaryatra takes that later and makes it now — for nine days, across four of the places in India that have spent centuries being honest about what a life is for.

The route

Four stops. The direction is always inward.

Days 1–2 · Stillness

Rishikesh

In the Himalayan foothills, where the Ganga is still young and fast. Mornings of yoga and quiet, evenings by the river. The first two days aren't about answers — they're about slowing down enough to hear the questions.

  • Dawn practice above the river, before the town wakes
  • An evening walk across Lakshman Jhula as the lamps come on
  • Night one: the first conversation
Days 3–4 · Letting go

Bodh Gaya

Where the Buddha sat beneath a tree until he stopped running from himself. Long, silent sits in the Mahabodhi gardens. This is where the trip stops being comfortable, and starts being useful.

  • Morning silence beneath the descendants of the Bodhi tree
  • Circling the temple at dusk with pilgrims from a dozen countries
  • An evening on what you're still carrying
Days 5–6 · Confluence

Prayagraj

At the Triveni Sangam, three rivers meet — one of them said to be invisible. A quiet boat out to the confluence, and a question worth sitting with: which parts of your own life have stopped speaking to each other?

  • A rowing boat out to where the waters change colour
  • Gulls, pilgrims, and a horizon with no edges
  • An evening on the parts of you that drifted apart
Days 7–9 · Death and rebirth

Varanasi

The oldest living city on earth. Sunrise on the Ganges, the ghats, the evening aarti at Dashashwamedh. Three days in the one place India has always been most honest about mortality — and, because of that, about how to actually live.

  • On the water before sunrise, the city waking behind you
  • Fire, bells and river at the evening aarti
  • Night nine: the last conversation

The shape of a day

Unhurried on purpose.

No itinerary crammed to the minute. Each day keeps the same simple spine, in every city — enough structure to hold you, enough space to let things surface.
